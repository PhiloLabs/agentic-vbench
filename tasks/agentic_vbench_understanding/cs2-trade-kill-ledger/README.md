# cs2-trade-kill-ledger

Reconstruct every **trade episode** of one full CS2 competitive match from ten
time-aligned, HUD-stripped first-person POV renders. Proposal and review: issue
#52, PR #94.

A trade episode = an initial kill A→B, plus the earliest revenge kill of A by a
teammate of B within 5.0 s in the same round. Each of the match's **30 episodes**
is scored only when round + both killers + both victims + both weapons are exact
and both timestamps are within 2.0 s.

## Why trade episodes (not a flat kill ledger)

An earlier design scored all 169 kills as a flat ledger. Two independent AI
reviews and an independent GPT-5 Codex design pass all flagged the same weakness:
it is cherry-pickable — an agent needs only ~9 easy, obvious kills to exceed the
0.10 hardness bar. Scoring only trade episodes fixes this: each item couples two
kills across three POVs, and a partial (one kill, or a wrong weapon) earns
nothing, so credit requires completing a whole causal pair. With 30 episodes, 1
complete episode = F1 0.065 and 2 = 0.13, so < 0.10 means a strong agent completes
fewer than ~1.5 episodes.

A simpler single-POV "own timeline" variant was prototyped and **measured too
easy** (Codex 0.31): the POV owner's own kills/deaths are the most observable
thing in his own video, his deaths are near-free to locate from the death-sound
audio, and round-counting is free from freeze-time silence. Making a single-POV
task hard requires opponent identity, which is unobservable from one POV — so the
valid hard task is the multi-POV, identity-bearing episode reconstruction. See
`calibration/scores.md`.

## Provenance

Source: the author's own private matchmaking demo (`.dem`, de_cache, MR12, 23
rounds, 169 player-vs-player kills, 30 trade episodes). `provenance/build_gt.py`
parses it with `demoparser2`, derives the kills/teams/weapons/trade relation
deterministically, emits `steps/solve/tests/gt_episodes.json` and the oracle, and
asserts `verifier(oracle)==1.0` / `verifier(empty)==0.0`. Conventions fixed in
code: kills exclude the one `worldent` fall death and any suicide/teamkill;
round-number is boundary-based (aftermath kills stay in the round just played);
the trade window is strictly `0 < Δt ≤ 5.0 s`, same round, killer on the victim's
team, earliest qualifying revenge. `provenance/derive_episodes.py` is the
standalone reference and was validated to reproduce all 30 episodes from the
committed ledger with zero mismatches.

The committed `gt_episodes.json` is currently the **rounds 1-2 pilot** (3 episodes
with real weapons, from the CSDM r1-2 parse) so the scorer is demonstrable now; the
full 30-episode GT is regenerated by `build_gt.py` on the render machine (the
`.dem` never leaves it) once the ten POVs and their exact `t0_tick` are final.

Independent GT cross-check: `provenance/csdm_crosscheck.json` records the CSDM
render-side parse vs demoparser2 agreeing 16/16 on rounds 1-2.

## Privacy and rights

Other players anonymized to `P1`-`P10`; `provenance/player_map.json` (steamid →
label) is gitignored; the `.dem` never ships. Code/verifier: Apache-2.0. Video:
CS2 gameplay footage under Valve's Video Policy (non-commercial, no separated game
assets, music kit and player voices disabled at record time).

## Camera coverage and identity

Ten first-person POVs, not one spectator camera, so coverage is structural: each
kill's initial victim is observable in the victim's own POV (defined through the
instant of death), and each killer's kill is observable in the killer's own POV.
Identity is the video index (`P<k>.mp4` = player `P<k>`), so there is no
name-label compositing and no occlusion rule. After a player dies his video is not
his own view until the next round; the instruction states this so an agent does
not read kills from a dead player's footage.

## Scoring

`steps/solve/tests/judge.py` (pure stdlib). Predictions are matched one-to-one to
GT episodes by maximum-cardinality bipartite matching; a match requires round
exact, both kills' killer/victim/weapon exact, and both timestamps within 2.0 s.
Reward = F1. Robust to malformed JSON and non-finite numbers (they score, not
crash). Anchors in `calibration/scores.md`.

## Render and pilot plan

Full render = ten full-match POVs × ~2.5 h each (~22.5 h remaining; P9 done). Gate
it with a cheap **pilot**: render all ten POVs of a dense short window (rounds 1-3
= 4 episodes on the natural clock, reusing P9 + the P3 clip; or rounds 18-20 = 7
episodes), ~5-7 h. Proceed to the full render only if, on the pilot, every field is
observable, a strong vision+audio agent scores < 0.10, and no audio-only or
on-screen shortcut reconstructs the episodes. Then upload the ten POVs to an
archive.org item and fill `environment/Dockerfile` (per-file SHA256).

Render pipeline notes (from the render machine): HLAE per-frame `spec_player` lock
is required — CSDM's one-shot player camera drifts off the target even while alive;
re-check player-voices-off and death-notice-duration=0 on every job (they silently
reset); lower graphics only after confirming weapon silhouettes / smoke / tracers
stay readable, and keep all ten files visually consistent.

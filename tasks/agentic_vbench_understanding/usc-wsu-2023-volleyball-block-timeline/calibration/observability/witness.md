that — but whether each name the scorer asks for can be **seen** in the shipped video.

## What this establishes

**The blockers are recoverable, on most events, by reading a number.** On 11 of 23
points every credited blocker is legible in the post-point window, and where a number
is legible it matches the answer key — no event was found where the video contradicts
the official record.

**The blocked hitter is not.** On 20 of 23 points no legible frame of the hitter exists
in that window at all. The reason is structural rather than incidental: the broadcast
cuts to whoever is celebrating, which is the blocking side, so the stuffed hitter is
usually out of frame or facing away. An agent that wants that field has to identify the
player from the rally itself — position, rotation and the attack it just watched —
rather than by reading a number after the whistle.

**Eight points have no legible attribution at all** in the window: two are set-winning
points where the feed cuts to the crowd or the changeover, and on the rest the camera
either stays wide or follows the team that did not make the block.

The scorer already treats the two fields asymmetrically: an answer with the blockers
exact and the hitter wrong earns partial credit rather than nothing. That is the tier
this evidence supports. Whether the hitter should be required for full credit at all is
a maintainer call, and the numbers above are the input to it.

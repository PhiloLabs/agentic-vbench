# Per-field witness — every credited name, on every event

Whether the answer key is right is settled by the official record the key cites. This
table answers a different question: whether each name the scorer asks for can be
**seen** in the shipped video.

For each of the 18 block points, `witness/<event>_terminal.jpg` holds eight frames
spanning the window around the score-bug reading (t-7.0, -5.5, -4.0, -2.5, +0.8, +1.6,
+2.4, +3.2 s), cropped to the band the net sits in and kept at the source's horizontal
resolution. The window runs to both sides because the cut to the post-point close-up is
not at a fixed offset from the bar: on set 3 at 14-18 the close-up is over before the
bar reads 14-18, on set 4 at 16-23 it comes after.

A name counts as **read** only when the digits are directly legible in the strip at
source resolution, without zooming or enhancement. In the wide sideline shot players
stand 40-60 px tall and numerals 10-20 px; glimpses at that size are recorded in the
notes and counted as not read.

Jersey colour disambiguates the six shared numbers: BYU wears royal blue with white
numerals and its libero white, Washington State crimson with white. Both teams have a
7, an 8, a 12, a 13, a 15 and a 21.

## What this establishes

**The blockers are recoverable on half the events by reading a number.** On 8 of 18
points every credited blocker is legible somewhere in the window, and on two more one
of the pair is. Where a number is legible it matches the answer key; no event was found
where the video contradicts the official record.

**The blocked hitter mostly is not.** On 12 of 18 points no legible frame of the hitter
exists in the window. The reason is structural rather than incidental: the feed cuts to
whoever is celebrating, which is the blocking side, so the stuffed hitter is usually out
of frame or facing away. The four that do read are the cases where the camera happens to
sit on the blocked team.

**Eight points have no credited blocker legible at all.** Three are windows where the
feed leaves the court entirely — the student section on set 2 at 10-10 and set 3 at 6-7,
the crowd on match point — and on the rest the camera either stays on the wide sideline
shot or follows the team that did not make the block. Set 3 at 14-18 is the opposite
trap: the post-bar close-up is of BYU 14, who is not credited on the play.

The scorer already treats the fields asymmetrically: an answer with the blockers exact
and the hitter wrong earns partial credit rather than nothing. That is the tier this
evidence supports. Whether the hitter should be required for full credit at all is a
maintainer call, and the numbers above are the input to it.
